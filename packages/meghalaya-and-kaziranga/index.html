East & Northeast

Root Bridges and Rhinos

The wettest hills in India, then the great one-horned rhino of Assam.

Shillong → Sohra → Dawki → Kaziranga

Duration
6 nights, 7 days
Best months
Oct–Apr
Pace
Active
Good for
Adventure, Nature lovers, Friends
Get a quote for this trip

The Northeast still feels like a discovery. In Meghalaya, Khasi villagers have grown bridges from living tree roots over generations, and rivers run so clear that boats seem to float on air. Then you drop to the plains of Assam, where the grasslands of Kaziranga hold the largest population of one-horned rhinoceros in the world.

The highlights

  • The double-decker root bridge at Nongriat
  • Nohkalikai Falls and the caves around Sohra
  • The clear water of the Umngot at Dawki
  • Mawlynnong village
  • Jeep safaris for rhino at Kaziranga

The story, day by day

  1. Day 1

    Guwahati to Shillong

    About three and a half hours from Guwahati, stopping at Umiam Lake on the way.

  2. Day 2

    To Sohra

    Drive to Sohra, also called Cherrapunji, with stops at Nohkalikai Falls, Mawsmai Cave and the Seven Sisters Falls.

  3. Day 3

    The root bridges

    The long stairway down to the double-decker bridge at Nongriat, with a swim in the pools if you like, and the climb back.

  4. Day 4

    Mawlynnong and Dawki

    Visit Mawlynnong village, see the single root bridge at Riwai, and take a boat on the Umngot at Dawki before returning to Shillong.

  5. Day 5

    Down to Assam

    About six to seven hours from the hills to the edge of Kaziranga National Park.

  6. Day 6

    Kaziranga

    Morning and afternoon jeep safaris through the grasslands for rhino, wild buffalo and birdlife.

  7. Day 7

    Back to Guwahati

    About four and a half hours to Guwahati, stopping at Kamakhya Temple if time allows, then your flight or train.
