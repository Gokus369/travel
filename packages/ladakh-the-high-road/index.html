The Himalaya

Ladakh, the High Road

Monasteries, high passes and two of the great landscapes of the Himalaya.

Leh → Khardung La → Nubra Valley → Pangong Tso

Duration
7 nights, 8 days
Best months
May–Sep
Pace
Active
Good for

Get a quote for this trip

Ladakh feels like the roof of another world: bare mountains in every shade of ochre, white monasteries on rock spurs, and a lake so blue it looks unreal. This route is paced for altitude first. You spend three days around Leh before crossing the high passes to the sand dunes of Nubra and the long blue water of Pangong Tso.

The highlights

  • Three days of careful acclimatisation around Leh
  • Thiksey and Hemis monasteries
  • Crossing Khardung La, well above 5,000 metres
  • Sand dunes and Bactrian camels at Hunder
  • Sunrise at Pangong Tso

The story, day by day

  1. Day 1

    Land in Leh

    Pick-up from the airport and straight to your hotel. Today you rest, drink water and let your body adjust.

  2. Day 2

    Easy Leh

    A short day: Shanti Stupa, Leh Palace and an unhurried walk around the market.

  3. Day 3

    The Indus Valley

    Visit the monasteries at Thiksey and Hemis and the palace at Shey.

  4. Day 4

    Over Khardung La

    Cross the pass to Nubra, see the giant Maitreya Buddha at Diskit, and spend the evening at the Hunder dunes.

  5. Day 5

    The Shyok road to Pangong

    A long and spectacular day of six to seven hours along the Shyok River to Pangong Tso. Overnight in a lakeside camp.

  6. Day 6

    Back over Chang La

    Watch the lake change colour at sunrise, then cross Chang La back to Leh, about five to six hours.

  7. Day 7

    Sham Valley

    An easier day to Gurudwara Pathar Sahib, Magnetic Hill and the Indus and Zanskar confluence.

  8. Day 8

    Departure

    Early transfer to Leh airport. Morning flights are the norm here.

Good to know: Leh sits at about 3,500 metres and the passes go much higher. The slow first days are not optional. Please check with your doctor if you have a heart or lung condition. Permit rules for Nubra and Pangong change from time to time, so we confirm them for your dates.
